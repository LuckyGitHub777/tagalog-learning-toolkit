# Content Notes

## Editorial posture

Tagalog Academy separates:

1. Source curriculum
2. Editorial interpretation
3. Public learning activities
4. Educator-approved language

The public site does not silently reproduce entire branded source documents. It transforms curriculum topics into neutral vocabulary, practice, builders, quizzes, and missions.

## Important language decisions

### Week 1

- `Kumusta?` is the primary spelling; `Kamusta?` is accepted in practice.
- `Ayos lang ako.` and `Okay lang ako.` are both recognized.
- `Nai-stress ako.` is taught as a fuller Tagalog construction; `Stressed ako.` is recognized as common Taglish.
- `Nag-aaral ako ng ____.` is used for a field or subject.
- Family terms distinguish conversational and formal alternatives such as `Nanay / Ina` and `Tatay / Ama`.

### Week 2

- Common borrowed color words are retained because they are widely used.
- `Berde / luntian` and `asul / bughaw` are presented as alternatives without claiming identical frequency in every context.
- Color linkers are introduced with examples such as `pulang kotse`, `berdeng sapatos`, and `asul na bahay`.
- `Gusto ko ng` may express liking or wanting depending on context.

### Week 3

- `Nasaan ang...` is used with common nouns.
- `Nasaan si...` is used with a personal name.
- `Nasa + place` gives a location.
- `Salamin` is labeled as mirror with a note that it can also mean eyeglasses.
- One source slide deck was titled for children even though the workbook was for adults; only aligned language content was retained.

### Week 4

- The original workbook label emphasized fruits and friends, but the materials also cover numbers, prices, relationships, and market dialogue. The public week title reflects the full scope.
- `Ilan?` asks how many by itself; `ilang` appears before a noun.
- Number linkers are introduced through forms such as `limang mangga` and `dalawampung piso`.
- `Kababayan` is defined broadly as someone from the same country, town, or community.
- `Magkano lahat?` is used as a natural alternative to the Taglish phrase `Magkano ang total?`.
- The malformed source prompt `Ilang ang katrabaho/kaklase mo?` was not reproduced.

## Review requirement

Language content should be reviewed by a qualified Tagalog educator for:

- Register and politeness
- Regional variation
- Naturalness
- Pronunciation guidance
- Linker explanations
- Accepted-answer variants
- English gloss precision

Do not describe the course as complete, certified, or authoritative until that review is documented.

## v3.0.1 language decisions

- Week 4 now displays the assimilated number spellings `labindalawa`, `labintatlo`, `labinlima`, `labimpito`, `labingwalo`, and `labinsiyam`; typed recall uses these forms.
- Week 1 displays `Na-stress ako.` and accepts `nai-stress ako` and `stressed ako` as documented variants pending educator review.
- The course uses `kwarto` as its display convention while accepting `kuwarto` in typed recall.
- `Dalandan` is identified as a citrus fruit; `kahel` is the Week 2 color term.
